# LinkedIn best practices (researched 2 Nov 2026)

LinkedIn does not publish its ranking rules. Almost every source below is a marketing vendor blog, and the numbers disagree with each other. Treat the figures as directional. Where sources conflict, the rule here takes the cautious side. Re-check this file every quarter.

## Evidence strength

| Practice | Evidence | Confidence |
|---|---|---|
| Front-load the hook before the "see more" fold | Many sources agree; exact length varies (about 100 to 210 characters, shorter on mobile) | High on the idea, low on the number |
| Short paragraphs, one idea per line | Consistent across sources | High |
| Dwell time and comments matter more than likes | Consistent across sources; thresholds differ | Medium |
| Document carousels hold attention | Vendor claims of 3x to 6x engagement, unverified | Medium |
| External links reduce reach, mostly on company pages | Large third-party study found about 26.5% less reach; LinkedIn says no deliberate penalty | Medium |
| Company pages reach far fewer people than personal profiles | Sources agree on direction; sizes differ widely | Medium |
| Early comments (first hour) help distribution | Sources agree; the exact window is disputed (90 minutes to 8 hours) | Low to medium |
| Unedited AI writing performs worse | Claimed by some sources, unverified | Low, but a human edit is required here anyway |

## Rules the writing skill applies

### Hook
- The key point lands in the first 140 characters. The fold is about 210 on desktop and shorter on mobile.
- Open with a concrete situation, a question the buyer actually asks, or a specific claim. Not "We are excited to announce".
- No opening hashtag or emoji.
- Write 3 hook options, then pick one.

### Body
- 150 to 300 words for a text post. Up to 400 only if every line earns its place.
- One idea per post.
- One to three sentences per paragraph. Blank line between paragraphs.
- Plain words. Explain any technical term once.
- Use a short list for steps or checks, with at most 5 items.
- One concrete example, step or number per post.

### Ending and CTA
- One CTA only, matched to the pillar: request a site survey, download the checklist, or book a call.
- A question to invite comments is allowed, but only if it is easy to answer in one line and the CTA still stands out. Do not use engagement bait such as "comment YES".

### Links
- Default: no external link in the post body. Evidence says link posts reach fewer people, and the penalty seems to fall on company pages.
- Put the link in the first comment or a reply, or point to a native document or form. Flag this for Amit's review.
- Use a body link only when clicks matter more than reach, and say so in the review note.

### Format
- Text post: default for opinions and short lessons.
- Document carousel: use for checklists and step-by-step processes. 6 to 10 slides, a hook slide, one idea per slide, mobile-first text size, one CTA on the last slide.
- Image or diagram: 4:5 portrait. Annotated diagrams beat stock photos.
- Native video: only when real footage exists.

### Hashtags and style
- Up to three hashtags at the end. Examples: #waterproofing #construction #UAE.
- No more than one emoji, and only if it adds meaning.
- No em dashes.
- No tagging people or companies without Amit's approval.

### Company page specifics
- Posts come from the company page. The page earns less organic reach than personal profiles, so add a review note suggesting staff reshare with their own comment.
- Post at a steady cadence. Two or three a week is the target in `brand/content-pillars.md`.
- Reply to comments within the first hour when a human is available. The agent can draft replies.
